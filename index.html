Why this exists

Getting sick far from home is a logistics problem first

Bali received 6.9 million international visitors in 2025. Most arrive without a doctor, without a local pharmacy, and without the language to navigate either one.

When something goes wrong, the hard part is rarely the medicine. It is knowing who to call, whether they speak English, whether the prescription is valid, and how long anything will take.

  • Consultations by video

    Licensed, English-speaking doctors, usually within the hour.

  • Medication delivered

    Same-day delivery to hotels, villas and homes, including prescription medicines.

  • Licensed local partners

    Clinics and pharmacies we work with directly, not a directory of links.

  • One case, start to finish

    The consultation, the prescription and the delivery stay in one place.

Background

Engineer first, founder second

I have been building businesses across Indonesia and Europe for about two decades. My foundation is technical: a Bachelor of Computer Science and a Bachelor of Engineering from BINUS University, double major in Industrial Engineering and Information Systems, graduated with High Merit.

Before BaliDoc I built DePaC Group, a multi-vertical business across food, hospitality, services and fashion retail, which grew revenue 300% in three years.

In 2017 I moved to the Netherlands for an MBA at Maastricht University, specialising in digital economy and business analytics. A graduate internship at a medical equipment company is where the idea behind BaliDoc started.

Since 2020 I have been a senior systems architect at LABB Consulting, building regulated digital infrastructure for Rabobank, de Volksbank and ASN Bank: KYC compliance, mortgage management, client lifecycle and investment onboarding. That work is the reason I take licensing, audit trails and data handling seriously at BaliDoc.

The best markets are built on solving real problems at scale. Bali's healthcare gap was not only a problem. It was an underserved market of millions.
